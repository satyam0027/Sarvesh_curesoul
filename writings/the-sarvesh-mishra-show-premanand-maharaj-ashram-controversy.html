What is the Premanand Maharaj ashram controversy?

The Premanand Maharaj ashram controversy has become one of the most discussed topics online. Thousands travel to Vrindavan to hear him, and millions watch his discourses on social media. So when a death linked to his ashram came to light, the reaction was immediate and loud.

Premanand Maharaj is among the most followed spiritual voices in India today. People bring him questions about love, family, grief, death and how to live. That reach is why anything connected to his ashram spreads so fast.

The controversy began with the death of a young disciple. From there, related claims — some verified and some not — began circulating online. Search results and trending videos mix confirmed details with speculation, which makes it hard for an ordinary reader to separate the two.

What happened to Deepak Lodhi (Rasik Dulari Sharan)?

According to the reports discussed in the video, Deepak Lodhi, known in the ashram as Rasik Dulari Sharan, died on 25 September 2026 after falling from the fourth floor of an ashram building in Vrindavan. Reports describe him as 25 years old.

The sequence reported so far:

  • The initial police angle. Police began by examining the possibility of suicide.
  • New questions. The postmortem reportedly noted multiple injuries, and a grinder was reportedly found in his room.
  • Forensic investigation. Forensic teams began examining the scene and related evidence.
  • The family’s concerns. Relatives raised serious questions about the circumstances.
  • The CCTV footage. A clip reportedly shows him sitting on a rooftop for about 11 seconds, then standing up and falling.

Police are still piecing together the full sequence. Until they publish findings, every one of these details is a part of the picture, not the whole picture.

What has the ashram said?

The Shri Radha Keli Kunj Trust has stated that it is cooperating fully with the police and has appealed to people not to spread unverified rumours. Search summaries also report that Premanand Maharaj, for health reasons, had already moved from Keli Kunj to Yamuna Vihar Kunj, and that security and darshan arrangements at the ashram have since changed. Please verify these points against the latest official statements, as the situation is moving quickly.

Why is society quick to judge a saint?

This is the heart of Sarvesh Mishra’s video. He deliberately declines to say whether it was murder or suicide, or whether the ashram is guilty or innocent. His point is that investigation is the job of the police and the courts, while the public conversation has already jumped ahead.

He traces a pattern through history, always carefully noting that some accounts come from tradition and not verified records:

  • Lord Ram. In the Uttarkand tradition, public gossip about Sita reached the king’s personal life and led to her exile.
  • Kabir. He challenged ritual hypocrisy among Hindus and Muslims alike. Later saint traditions record resistance and hardship in his life.
  • Ravidas. He asked whether reaching God requires a ladder of birth. Traditional accounts describe conflict with the orthodox, though not every detail is verified history.
  • Dayanand Saraswati. He questioned idol worship, child marriage and caste discrimination. After his death, a poisoning allegation arose, but it was never proven in court.
  • Sri Aurobindo. Arrested in 1908 by the British, he was acquitted on 6 May 1909 and later became one of India’s great spiritual figures.

The medium changed from village talk to viral videos, but the instinct stayed the same: question first, assume next, accuse after, and discover the truth very late.

— Sarvesh Mishra, The Sarvesh Mishra Show Episode 32

Allegation vs proof: the difference that matters

An allegation is a claim. Proof is what survives scrutiny. The courtroom, the postmortem, the forensic report and the CCTV analysis exist to move a case from one to the other.

Sarvesh Mishra makes two points that sit side by side without contradiction:

Both can be true

A saint’s lifetime of practice cannot be reduced to a single allegation. An allegation cannot be buried simply because the accused is a saint.

Devotees should not fear questions, because truth does not need protection from scrutiny. Critics should not erase the line between suspicion and evidence.

How to read controversial news responsibly

  1. Check the source. Is it the police, a court, an official statement, or a social media post?
  2. Separate fact from claim. A postmortem finding is not the same as a viral caption.
  3. Watch for urgency. Headlines that demand an instant verdict are usually selling attention, not information.
  4. Wait for the investigation. Preliminary leads often change.
  5. Correct yourself. If you shared something that turns out false, say so as loudly as you first shared it.

Why this matters beyond one ashram

When opinion hardens before facts arrive, there are real costs. A guilty party may be shielded by noise, or an innocent person may be ruined by it. A grieving family may be buried under a debate that stopped being about their loss. And the public loses its ability to tell trustworthy reporting from performance.

Not a conclusion

This page does not decide murder, suicide, guilt or innocence. That is the investigation’s job. Reports cited here should be checked against later official findings.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Premanand Maharaj ashram controversy about?
It centres on the death of a young disciple, Deepak Lodhi (Rasik Dulari Sharan), on 25 September 2026 at the ashram in Vrindavan, and the questions raised by his family and the public.
Who was Rasik Dulari Sharan?
He was a young disciple, originally Deepak Lodhi, associated with the ashram. Reports describe him as 25 years old.
Has the police investigation concluded?
Not as of the latest reports. Police and forensic teams are still examining the case, so no final conclusion should be assumed.
What does the CCTV footage show?
Reports say it shows him sitting on a rooftop for about 11 seconds before standing and falling. Police are still assessing it with other evidence.
What has the ashram trust said?
The Shri Radha Keli Kunj Trust says it is fully cooperating with the police and has urged people to avoid spreading unverified rumours.
Is it true that celebrities paid money for darshan?
The claim circulated on social media and was reportedly denied by the celebrities’ representatives as baseless.
What about the families who said their relatives were missing?
According to reports, police located at least two of them safe elsewhere, with no link to Keli Kunj.
Is it wrong to question a saint?
No. Questions are legitimate and necessary. The problem begins when a question is treated as a verdict before evidence is examined.
Why does the video mention Ram, Kabir and Sri Aurobindo?
To show that saints have often been questioned or accused in their own time, and that allegation does not automatically equal truth.
