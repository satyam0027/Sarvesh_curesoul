Bitcoin, Crypto Rules in India, and How to Start Safely: Umesh Kumar (Sun Crypto) on The Sarvesh Mishra Show

Why This Crypto Conversation Matters

Crypto is one of the most talked-about and most misunderstood topics in personal finance. Is it legal in India? Is it safe? Is Bitcoin an investment or just speculation? What happens if an exchange shuts down?

In this episode of The Sarvesh Mishra Show, Sarvesh Mishra sits down again with Umesh Kumar, Founder & CEO of Sun Crypto. Their first conversation, over a year ago, drew hundreds of comments and dozens of viewer questions. This follow-up answers many of those questions in plain language, covering risk, benefit, and where to be careful. Read the show explainer at What Is The Sarvesh Mishra Show?

This conversation is distinct from the Show episodes with CA Nitin Murarka on options trading rules and Priyank Sharma on intraday trading traps.

Key Takeaways

  • Bitcoin is treated as an asset class, which the Indian government calls a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA).
  • Umesh says you can start with as little as ₹100, just as you would buy 1 gram of gold rather than a kilo.
  • In India, crypto is not illegal, but it is not legal tender either. You can trade and invest, but not use it as a payment mode.
  • Gains on spot crypto are taxed at 30% with 1% TDS, and losses are not offset.
  • Always use an FIU-registered platform.
  • Umesh says 80–90% of new entrants lose money, mainly from lack of knowledge, over-trading, and greed.
  • Futures and leverage can multiply gains and losses. Learn before you trade.

Why Was Bitcoin Demanded in the Strait of Hormuz Situation?

Sarvesh opens with a news item: when the Strait of Hormuz was blocked, the demand reportedly wasn't for dollars or gold but for crypto. Why?

Umesh explains it through control. Rupees are controlled by the RBI, and dollars and euros by their central authorities. These bodies can mint, burn, track, and freeze money, and bank accounts can be frozen during disputes.

Bitcoin is different. Once it reaches your wallet address, no government or authority can block or stop it. Demanding dollars would invite sanctions and pressure, and euros would be traceable. Bitcoin sits outside central control, which is why it makes sense in such a situation.

What Is Bitcoin? Asset, Technology, or Future Financial Infrastructure?

Umesh breaks it down into three angles:

  • Technology: Blockchain came first, and Bitcoin is built on it.
  • Origin: Bitcoin emerged around 2009, after the Lehman Brothers crash shook the financial system. It was first dismissed as a scam or Ponzi scheme.
  • Asset class: The Indian government's tax rules define it as a Virtual Digital Asset. Other countries treat it as a security or an asset.

Bitcoin as "Digital Gold"

Umesh calls Bitcoin digital gold because its supply is limited, unlike gold, where new supply keeps entering the market.

Bitcoin vs. Other Cryptocurrencies

There are 16,000+ cryptocurrencies, with Bitcoin at the top. Umesh's view:

  • Top coins like Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana are less speculative and are the "blue chips" of crypto.
  • Smaller coins can swing 50–60% in a day and are more open to manipulation.
  • For long-term goals, avoid speculative coins and keep to the safer end.

Bitcoin's market cap is over $1.5 trillion (Umesh notes the exact figure should be verified), and large institutions like BlackRock hold or offer Bitcoin ETFs.

Fiat Money vs. Decentralized Crypto

Central banks can print, burn, track, and freeze fiat money. Bitcoin, by contrast, is controlled by its holders, whether they own 2 Bitcoin or a tiny fraction of one. This is the core of the "decentralization" idea.

The Hawala Question: Is Crypto a Tool for Illegal Money Movement?

Sarvesh raises the common concern that crypto enables hawala and harms the country. Umesh's response:

  • Hawala began with cash, which leaves no trail unless a camera happens to record it.
  • Crypto transactions are recorded permanently on a public blockchain ledger. Any third party can trace funds from wallet A to B to C to D.
  • So while misuse exists (as with any financial tool), the trail can never be erased, and with proper tools and policy every transaction can be monitored.

Umesh doesn't deny that crypto is used in illegal activity. He argues that traceability is a strength regulators can use.

Global Regulation vs. India's Regulatory Landscape

Around the world

The United States is moving toward legalization, with a major legislative act (the Clarity Act) said to be in the approval process. UAE, Hong Kong, Singapore, Thailand, and Japan have regulations in place, and Bhutan's government reportedly holds Bitcoin itself. In Thailand, crypto ATMs and payments already work.

India's journey

  • Around 2014–15, the RBI signaled that crypto was a scam or pyramid scheme.
  • In 2017–18, the RBI banned banks from dealing with crypto exchanges, creating panic. The Supreme Court later struck down the ban.
  • Crypto was then brought under PMLA, with transaction monitoring.
  • The government introduced 30% tax and 1% TDS.
  • Guidelines for exchanges followed, including KYC. A full licensing and regulatory framework is the expected next step.

Umesh compares this to the internet and UPI. Had India rejected the internet over fraud fears, UPI would not exist today.

Crypto Taxation in India: 30% Tax and 1% TDS

Umesh's explanation of the tax structure, in simple terms:

  • Holding crypto doesn't attract tax. Tax applies when you sell or convert it.
  • The 30% tax applies to profit only, not the full amount. If ₹1 lakh becomes ₹2 lakh, you pay 30% on the ₹1 lakh gain.
  • 1% TDS is deducted by the exchange, like TDS on bank interest.
  • Losses don't attract tax, but they can't be set off against other income.
  • On futures and options (leveraged trading), Umesh says gains are treated as business income and taxed by slab rather than at the flat 30% rate. This is why many active traders prefer that route.

Important: Tax rules can change and depend on individual circumstances. Please confirm with a qualified Chartered Accountant before making decisions. Related: CA Nitin Murarka on options trading rules.

Sarvesh points out that a 30% tax and TDS regime signals that the government does not treat crypto as banned. Umesh agrees, though he adds that this is not the same as formal approval as an investment product. He describes India as moving steadily from bans toward regulation: exchange guidelines, taxation, and now investor-protection guidelines.

Exchange Safety: FIU Registration, Cold Storage, and Insurance

What is the FIU? The Financial Intelligence Unit is a government body that monitors financial transactions, including on crypto platforms. Umesh advises users to choose only FIU-registered exchanges.

What if an exchange disappears? Umesh acknowledges that exchange failures and hacks have happened globally, and some platforms didn't survive. For Sun Crypto, he says:

  • Customer crypto is held in cold storage, on devices disconnected from the internet.
  • Holdings are insured up to $150 million, according to Umesh.
  • The platform has been running for 6+ years with 30 lakh+ users.

How to choose an exchange (Umesh's checklist)

  • Is it authorized and compliant, and how long has it operated?
  • Who are the founders and how active is the team?
  • Is the app simple and easy to use?
  • Does it have insurance, and has it been hacked before?
  • Does it provide learning support and risk tools?
  • Does it charge monthly or maintenance fees?

Starting With ₹100

"Bitcoin is ₹80 lakh, I can't afford that." Umesh says this misconception stops many people. You don't buy a whole Bitcoin, just as you don't buy a kilo of gold. You can buy a fraction, starting from about ₹100.

According to Umesh, opening an account takes roughly two minutes with PAN and Aadhaar, and you can add money through UPI. He says Sun Crypto charges no account-opening or maintenance fees, earns a very small brokerage on trades, and offers new users up to ₹250 in free Bitcoin with conditions. Related: Sachin Jain on the ₹1 crore SIP plan and Sanjay Kathuria on SIP and wealth.

Investing vs. Trading in a 24/7 Market

Investing: a long-term view where you build gradually, for example ₹100 now and more next month.

Trading: a short-term approach aiming for daily returns. Because crypto runs 24/7, working professionals can trade at 10 PM or 3 AM, unlike traditional markets with fixed hours.

Spot, futures, and leverage

  • Spot: Pay ₹10,000, get ₹10,000 of Bitcoin. Gains and losses follow the price.
  • Futures with leverage: With ₹10,000 and 10x leverage, you control ₹1 lakh of Bitcoin. A 1% move changes your result by ₹1,000 either way.
  • Going short: In futures you can also profit when prices fall.
  • Risk tools: Stop-loss and take-profit orders help protect capital.

Umesh is clear that leverage cuts both ways. Losses can be as fast and as large as gains, and you're responsible for your own decisions. Related: Deepak Wadhwa on option trading risk and Priyank Sharma on intraday trading traps.

Trading Psychology: FOMO, Panic Selling, and Over-Trading

Asked which mindset is most dangerous, Umesh names over-trading and FOMO:

  • After a small loss, people take 10 more trades to recover it and lose even more.
  • When prices fall for days, even long-term holders panic-sell, often near the bottom.
  • After hitting a daily target, people keep trading out of greed and break their own rules.

His advice: set your target, book your profit, and stay disciplined. Related: Coach Jitu Sharma on money mindset and NLP.

Portfolio Thinking: How Much Should You Put in Crypto?

Umesh's personal approach is to keep roughly 20% of investments in crypto, alongside real estate, stocks, and gold. Because crypto is subject to market risk, he says to size your allocation by how much loss you can truly afford. He also advises against relying only on past Bitcoin cycles, since regulation and global developments keep changing the picture. Related: Avneesh on real estate investment.

Risks of Foreign Exchanges

Umesh warns that many Indians use offshore platforms that don't follow Indian rules. He cites a recent report of 18+ foreign platforms being blocked in India, leaving users to withdraw funds within a notice period. He also notes that tax obligations may still catch up with users of such platforms. His advice is to stay with compliant, domestic exchanges.

He adds that trading volumes on Indian exchanges are down 70–80%, which he attributes to the tax and compliance burden pushing traders toward offshore platforms.

Stablecoins (USDT, USDC) and the Future of Payments

Are stablecoins only for trading? Umesh says no:

  • They're increasingly used for cross-border payments and remittances.
  • Bank transfers can take 2–3 days and cost 2–3% in charges, while stablecoin transfers can arrive in minutes.
  • Licenses for such services already exist in places like Canada, the US, and Dubai.

Umesh mentions that a group of large US banks is building stablecoin infrastructure.

Will crypto replace UPI? Umesh says no. UPI is backed by banks and the RBI and is nearly free, while blockchain transactions carry costs. Crypto works better for investing, trading, and cross-border transfers. In India, crypto is not legal tender, so it can be traded and invested in but not used for payments.

Final Advice for Beginners

Umesh ends with a frank statement: 80–90% of new market entrants lose money, and the main reason is lack of knowledge, followed by over-trading and greed. His advice:

  • Learn before you trade, and start small.
  • Use demo or paper trading to practice without real money.
  • Choose safe, compliant platforms.
  • Never risk money you can't afford to lose.

Sarvesh closes with a caution of his own: listen to every guest, do your own research, use AI and Google, read feedback, and only then decide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is crypto legal in India?
Crypto is not banned. It is taxed, and exchanges must follow guidelines. It is not legal tender, so it can't be used as a payment mode.
What is the minimum amount to start investing in Bitcoin?
According to Umesh Kumar, you can start from ₹100 on Sun Crypto.
How is crypto taxed in India?
As discussed in the episode: 30% on profit for spot gains, and 1% TDS. Confirm current rules with a CA.
What does FIU-registered mean?
It means the platform is registered with the Financial Intelligence Unit and follows KYC and anti-money-laundering guidelines.
Can crypto replace UPI?
Umesh believes not. It suits investing, trading, and cross-border payments better.

Conclusion

The follow-up conversation between Sarvesh Mishra and Umesh Kumar treats Bitcoin as a regulated asset class in India, not as legal tender and not as a guaranteed investment. The practical thread is to learn first, start small, use an FIU-registered platform, and confirm tax rules with a Chartered Accountant.
