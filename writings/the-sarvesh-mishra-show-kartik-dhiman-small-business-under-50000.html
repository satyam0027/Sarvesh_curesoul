Most beginners wait for a big investment before they start. The better move, says Kartik Dhiman on The Sarvesh Mishra Show, is to start with what you can afford today. This guide covers small business ideas under 50000 that need little money, no factory and no fancy office. It is built on examples he shared with Sarvesh Mishra.

Why small business ideas under 50000 work best as services

A product needs stock, packaging and storage. A service needs your time and a plan. That is why Kartik Dhiman tells beginners to start with service businesses.

A real example from the episode: a trip planner charges about ₹2,500 per person and takes 10 people to a popular place like Khatu Shyam Ji or Ujjain. That is ₹25,000 collected, usually in advance, before most costs are paid. Stranger group trips to places like Manali are also trending on Instagram.

Low investment business ideas he recommends

1. Travel agency (service business)

This is his top pick for people with little capital. You do not need a product or a heavy licence to begin. Pick a destination your city already travels to, such as Khatu Shyam Ji or Ujjain Mahakaleshwar, plan the trip, and take a group. He gives an example of charging around ₹2,500 per person for a group of 10, with payments coming in advance. Stranger group trips to places like Manali, priced around ₹3,000, are going viral on Instagram.

2. Influencer marketing agency

Do not become the creator. Earn from creators. Regional creators are growing fast, from Haryanvi vloggers to village content makers, and brands need them. The model is commission based. A brand has a budget, you find creators who will work within a lower number, and you keep the difference. Start by building a database in Google Sheets, reach out to brands and deliver on time.

3. Kulhad manufacturing

He believes North India has no big kulhad brand and demand is rising. Kulhads have moved from tea to lassi, chaat, rajma and desserts. A plain tea sells for ₹10, a kulhad tea for ₹20, while the kulhad itself costs around ₹12 according to him. This one needs more capital than a service business.

4. Jaggery (gur) and organic products

People are avoiding refined sugar, and jaggery is a natural alternative. Kartik points out that Tata has launched a jaggery brand. Organic spices and groundnut oil follow the same logic, as more food brands move away from palm oil.

5. Mitti based village businesses

Multani mitti, kulhads and other earth based products can be started from a village. His view is that rural India holds hidden business ideas, and rural life teaches you how customers and shopkeepers actually behave.

6. Swimming and wellness services

His prediction is that after gyms, Zumba and yoga, people will move to swimming for weight loss. This needs a larger budget, so it is not a ₹50,000 idea, but it is a trend to watch.

7. Large import substitution ideas

For bigger ambitions, he named titanium dioxide and carbon as chemicals India imports heavily. These need serious capital and research, so treat them as long term ideas rather than starter ideas.

How to start

  1. Know your strengths
  2. Pick one idea from the table
  3. Check market size and CAGR on Google
  4. Test demand with 10 real people
  5. Take advance payments or small orders
  6. Register, then deliver on time
  7. Write simple SOPs, then grow

Basics before you spend a rupee

Register your business through Udyam Registration and check GST rules on the GST portal. If you plan to sell jaggery, spices or oil, you need a licence from FSSAI. The government’s Startup India site lists schemes for beginners.

Common beginner mistakes

Kartik warns about three traps. First, spending most of your money on a fancy office instead of your business. Second, copying social media gurus who promise easy lakhs. Third, doing everything yourself. Write a simple SOP, meaning a written step by step process, before you hire anyone.

He also says part time effort usually brings part time results, so treat your idea seriously from week one.

Why most small business ideas fail before they start

Kartik makes a bold claim. Doing a job and a business together usually fails, because a job is more than eight hours of work. It is a mindset of “I will give exactly eight hours and not one minute more.”

A business has no clock. Founders plan at midnight, fix problems at 2 AM and think about growth in the shower. If you can only work in fixed hours, the market will not wait for you.

His advice is blunt. If you are serious, be ready to take the first risk, which is leaving the job. If you cannot, he suggests not starting at all. You may disagree, and many people successfully build side income, but his point is worth sitting with. Part time effort tends to produce part time results.

How to choose the right business idea

Kartik uses two filters.

Filter one: know the person. He spends the first half hour asking about your family, studies, habits and interests. Someone who loves food may belong in food. Someone whose brother runs a clothing business may understand clothing better than anyone. A creative person who gets bored easily should avoid manufacturing, which he calls a pure discipline business.

Filter two: check the market. Search for “[your product] market size in India” and look at the CAGR. His rule of thumb is that a market above ₹2 lakh crore offers room to build a very large business, while a smaller market can still make you a good living if your goal is modest.

Why founders get stuck at ₹10 to ₹20 lakh a month

Revenue is not the same as profit, and neither is the same as a business. Kartik lists three common reasons founders stall.

  • Small vision. If you are happy at ₹10 lakh, you will stay at ₹10 lakh.
  • No systems. Without SOPs and KPIs, the founder is stuck doing everything. He recommends writing the SOP before hiring anyone.
  • No department heads. One head per team, and for multiple states, one head per state.

His three stages of business are revenue, profit and automation. A business is healthy when it keeps earning, even at half strength, when the founder is away for 15 to 20 days.

Beware of social media business gurus

Kartik and Sarvesh both warn about people who sell the dream of easy lakhs. Ask any coach what they have built, how they earn and where your fee goes. He also advises founders to be careful about excessive publicity, since one controversy can damage a business built over years.

Key takeaways
  • Part time rarely builds a real business. Commitment decides the outcome.
  • Service businesses such as travel and influencer agencies need the least capital.
  • Check market size and CAGR before you pick an idea.
  • Packaging, product quality and marketing all matter, in that order of first impression.
  • Write SOPs and KPIs before hiring.
  • Measure your business by whether it earns without you.
  • Be careful with easy income claims online.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best small business ideas under 50000?

Group travel planning and influencer agencies need the least money.

Can a complete beginner start with ₹50,000?

Yes, if you choose a service and take advance payments. That is his view, not a guarantee.

Do I need a licence?

Udyam registration is a good start. Food products need FSSAI.

How do I find customers?

Make content about your work on Instagram and YouTube from day one.

How long until I earn?

It depends on your idea and effort. Test with 10 customers first.

Should I quit my job first?

Kartik says yes for serious growth, but only take that risk if your finances allow.
