Can the way you eat, chew your food and manage your day influence health? On The Sarvesh Mishra Show, Sarvesh Mishra sits with Sweekar Bhardwaj on Type 2 diabetes, thyroid disorders and PCOD — plus mindful eating, fasting, stress and sleep. The question in the room: are we paying attention to habits, or only to medicines?

Key takeaways from the conversation
  • Type 2 diabetes was discussed through insulin resistance — insulin as a “key” for glucose entering cells.
  • Diabetes is not only “from eating sugar”; rice, roti and other carbohydrates also affect blood glucose.
  • Chewing slowly can support mindful eating. Chewing exactly 32 times is not an established treatment.
  • 16:8 fasting was described as a popular pattern. It is not for everyone, especially people on glucose-lowering medicines.
  • Thyroid medicine should never be reduced or stopped without a doctor and tests.
  • PCOD/PCOS needs individual medical care, not random internet remedies.

1. Understanding diabetes: what happens inside the body?

Sweekar Bhardwaj explains insulin resistance with a simple analogy: insulin as a key that helps glucose enter cells for energy. When cells respond less well, glucose may stay elevated in the blood.

Lifestyle factors discussed: eating patterns, movement, calorie load, sleep, stress, body weight and metabolic health. An important distinction in the episode: diabetes is not simply caused by eating sugar. Carbohydrate-containing foods, including rice and roti, also contribute to blood glucose. Understanding how the body processes food is a step toward informed choices — not a substitute for a clinician’s plan. Related: Vaidya Sushil Dubey on sugar control (a different episode).

2. Insulin resistance and eating habits

He uses a guest-who-visits-too-often analogy for repeated insulin stimulation. The talk then moves to meal timing, eating frequency and individual nutritional needs. The takeaway: eating should not be completely random; a consistent routine can be part of metabolic care. Meal frequency and fasting still need to be individualised, especially if you have diabetes or take glucose-lowering medication.

3. Mindful eating and chewing food 32 times

Chewing slowly and taking smaller bites is a central theme. In his approach, eating slowly may help people notice hunger and fullness, pay attention to food, avoid rushing, and enjoy meals with fewer distractions. Sarvesh Mishra shares eating a sandwich slowly, taking about 35 minutes. Eating is not only what we consume; it is also how we consume it. Chewing exactly 32 times has not been established as a treatment for diabetes, thyroid disorders or PCOD.

4. Intermittent fasting and metabolic health

He describes the commonly discussed 16:8 window — eat in eight hours, fast sixteen — and talks about fasting, insulin activity and autophagy (cells recycling damaged components). The discussion asks viewers to understand the idea rather than copy a trend. Intermittent fasting is not suitable for everyone. People with diabetes, particularly those using insulin or certain glucose-lowering medicines, should seek medical guidance before changing meal timings.

5. Thyroid: hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism

The conversation notes thyroid disorders are particularly common among women.

Hypothyroidism

When the gland does not produce enough thyroid hormones. Symptoms discussed: unexplained weight gain, fatigue, constipation, muscle weakness, difficulty managing weight, menstrual irregularities.

Hyperthyroidism

When the gland produces too much. Symptoms discussed: unintentional weight loss, increased bowel movements, rapid heartbeat, anxiety, heat intolerance, menstrual irregularities.

Genetics, lifestyle and autoimmune conditions came up. He shares a personal account of a woman whose thyroid medication dosage reportedly reduced after health changes. Regular testing and medical supervision matter. Thyroid medication should never be reduced or discontinued without the treating doctor and appropriate thyroid function tests.

6. PCOD: hormonal health

They discuss how hormonal imbalance can affect women’s physical and emotional well-being. Symptoms mentioned in the PCOS/PCOD conversation: irregular cycles, acne, excess facial or body hair, difficulty with weight, fertility challenges, emotional stress and mood changes.

He also talks about mindful eating and gradual weight management as part of lifestyle habits. Women should not ignore symptoms or rely only on random internet remedies. PCOS is a complex hormonal and metabolic condition; management should be based on individual medical assessment.

7. Stress, sleep and physical health

Asked whether stress and poor sleep can contribute to conditions such as diabetes, the conversation points to the link between physical and emotional well-being. Chronic stress and insufficient sleep can influence hormonal regulation, appetite, blood glucose, energy and mood.

He describes meditation as noticing thoughts without immediately reacting: sit quietly, close the eyes, observe thoughts, avoid judging every one, practise a few minutes regularly. Chamomile tea is mentioned as a traditional relaxation option — not a prescription. Mental well-being is part of physical care, in this framing.

8. PCOD, weight and consistency

Later they return to PCOD and weight, including emotional load. Smaller bites, slower chewing and less dependence on highly processed foods come up again. Meaningful lifestyle change needs consistency. No single habit guarantees reversal of every hormonal or metabolic condition. Individualised medical care, nutrition and sustainable habits remain important.

9. Money, satisfaction and contributing

Toward the end, Sarvesh asks about money, family and society. Sweekar’s view: earning should not be the only purpose of life. Once genuine needs are met, additional wealth can go toward social work. He discusses wanting a space where people can learn healthier eating without financial pressure, plus honesty, gratitude, Vipassana and being satisfied with what one has. That is his perspective, not a financial plan.

10. The host’s close: health deserves attention

Sarvesh Mishra’s reminder: we spend a large part of life on money, success and property, none of which replace health. Become more conscious of everyday habits, understand the body, and take responsibility — including seeking appropriate medical guidance. Health should not wait until a crisis.

Frequently asked questions

What are the three major health conditions discussed?

Type 2 diabetes, thyroid disorders and PCOD, plus lifestyle factors that may influence overall health.

What is insulin resistance?

When cells respond less effectively to insulin, making it harder for glucose to enter cells and potentially raising blood glucose.

What is the 32-times chewing concept?

A mindful-eating approach: chew slowly and pay attention. There is no medically established requirement to chew exactly 32 times.

What is intermittent fasting?

An eating pattern that alternates eating and fasting. 16:8 is commonly discussed. Not suitable for everyone; ask a doctor if you take diabetes medicines.

What is the difference between hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism?

Hypothyroidism: insufficient thyroid hormones. Hyperthyroidism: excessive thyroid hormones.

What are some common symptoms of PCOD or PCOS?

Irregular periods, acne, excess facial hair, weight-related difficulties and fertility challenges can occur. See a doctor for assessment.

Can stress and poor sleep affect metabolic health?

The episode discusses how chronic stress and inadequate sleep can influence blood glucose, appetite, hormones and overall metabolic health.

Should people with diabetes stop medicine and try fasting?

No. Never discontinue medication or start fasting without medical supervision.

Why is mindful eating important, in this conversation?

It encourages slowing down, noticing hunger and fullness, and becoming more aware of food choices. It is not a cure.
