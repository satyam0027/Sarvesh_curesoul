Most parenting advice begins after a child is born — which school to pick, which books to read, which values to instil. In this episode of The Sarvesh Mishra Show, host Sarvesh Mishra sits down with Dr. Vishnu Mane, who has spent the last six years working with expecting parents on a practice he calls Garbh Sanskar, to make a different case entirely: that the nine months of pregnancy are, in his view, the most decisive window in a child's development.

Garbh Sanskar Kyu Hai Zaroori? (Why Does Garbh Sanskar Matter?)

Dr. Mane's central claim is straightforward — a large part of a child's brain, sensory organs, and what he calls "capacity" develops in the womb. What happens after birth, he argues, is mostly about using what was already shaped during pregnancy, not building it from scratch. This is not framed as an alternative to medical care, but as an additional, conscious layer of attention that he believes most expecting parents skip simply because they don't know it exists.

Garbh Sanskar Kya Hai? (What Is Garbh Sanskar?)

Dr. Mane frames Garbh Sanskar not as a religious ritual, but as a conscious daily practice — the mother staying happy, active, creative, energetic, peaceful, stable, and positive throughout pregnancy, supported by routines her partner and family help sustain. He's careful to separate this from devotional activity: in his words, Garbh Sanskar is often mistaken for pooja-paath, but he presents it instead as a repeatable, learnable practice.

Garbh Sanskar Kaise Kare? (How to Practice Garbh Sanskar)

A Definition, Not a Ritual

His own definition is precise — a pregnant mother staying happy, active, creative, energetic, peaceful, stable, positive, and consistent for all nine months of pregnancy. He draws a firm line between knowledge and practice: reading a book about Garbh Sanskar gives you information about it, he says, but it doesn't by itself constitute doing it. The practice comes from daily repetition over the full nine months, not a one-time class or workshop.

The Mother as "Designer"

A recurring metaphor in the conversation is the mobile phone. A phone's value isn't just its outer hardware, Dr. Mane points out — it's the operating system running inside it, invisible from the outside. He uses this to argue that a child's intellectual and emotional "software," described using terms like grasping ability, IQ, EQ, and processing capacity, is largely shaped during pregnancy, even though it isn't visible until much later in life.

Key Insight

The practice comes from daily repetition over the full nine months, not a one-time class or workshop. Knowledge ≠ practice.

Pregnancy Mein Kya Kare? (The Five Pillars of a Conscious Pregnancy)

Asked what actually influences a developing baby, Dr. Mane repeatedly returns to five factors:

  1. 1
    What You Eat

    Physical nutrition is the one area he says most Indian families are already reasonably aware of, through scans, supplements, and diet guidance from doctors.

  2. 2
    What You Watch

    Screen content, television, and social media consumption should, in his view, be chosen consciously rather than used purely to pass time.

  3. 3
    What You Read

    Books and material connected to the qualities parents hope to nurture in their child — his suggestion is to read with intention, not at random.

  4. 4
    What You Hear

    Music, conversations, and ambient sound at home. He references the idea that pregnancy-specific music can help calm the nervous system.

  5. 5
    Your Environment

    The overall emotional atmosphere at home, including the relationship between both parents — the factor he spends the most time on in the conversation.

Pita Ki Bhumika Kya Hai? (The Father's Role in Garbh Sanskar)

A significant part of the discussion focuses on conflict and stress between partners during pregnancy. Dr. Mane references research linking prenatal stress to elevated cortisol levels, and states that sustained parental stress during pregnancy is associated, in his understanding of that research, with slower growth patterns and long-term health outcomes for the child. These claims reflect his personal reading and professional experience rather than independently verified medical consensus.

He pushes back on the idea that this responsibility rests only on the mother. A father's role, he says, is twofold — the direct biological role, and an equally important indirect role of providing the emotional environment, much like a farmer provides water and nutrients to a planted seed.

"A mother doesn't just give birth to a son or a daughter. A mother gives birth to change itself."

— Dr. Vishnu Mane

Two Stories Dr. Mane Shares

The Father Who Turned His Home Into a Cricket Ground

Dr. Mane recounts meeting a father who had always dreamed of representing India in cricket but never got the chance. During his wife's pregnancy, this father consciously built the home environment around cricket — what they watched, discussed, and listened to. Today, Dr. Mane says, their daughter plays leather-ball cricket for a Maharashtra team, and reportedly remembers cricketer M.S. Dhoni's birthday more consistently than her own.

Jijabai and Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj

He also references the story of Jijabai, mother of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, as an example of intentional pregnancy. In his telling, amid political conflict within her family, Jijabai resolved that her child would grow up to build Hindavi Swarajya, and shaped her nine months around that purpose, including reading the Ramayana for the qualities of leadership it represents.

Garbh Sanskar Daily Routine Kaise Banaye? (A Sample Daily Routine for Expecting Parents)

Dr. Mane outlines a structured daily rhythm built around roughly 90 minutes spread across the day, rather than one long session.

Time of DaySuggested ActivityStated Purpose
Early morningAffirmations, gratitude practiceCalming the mind while it is least active
BreakfastPregnancy-specific music, visualisationReducing brain frequency, imagining the baby's development
Commute to work10–15 minutes of musicBuilding peace and stability
Work hoursCreative and logical tasksSupporting analytical and creative development
EveningSensory-organ-focused activitiesSupporting sensory development
Before bedVisualisation, breathing, affirmationsEnding the day in a positive, connected state
Key Principle

Consistency over intensity. Dr. Mane compares it to exercising once a week and expecting the results of a daily routine. Patterns that aren't repeated daily don't produce the same effect.

Pregnancy Mein Phone Ka Use Kitna Kare? (Screen Time During Pregnancy)

Asked directly about mobile phone use, Dr. Mane doesn't say to avoid screens altogether. His position is more specific: it's not the device that matters, but what's being consumed on it. He argues that content unrelated to the qualities a parent hopes to nurture is of little use, and that distressing content, including news, can create fear and anxiety during pregnancy. His suggestion is to consciously choose content aligned with the environment parents want to create.

3 Things Every Expecting Parent Should Keep in Mind

1. Conception should be a conscious decision. He describes confusion and ambivalence around a pregnancy as something a developing baby can, in his view, sense and be affected by.

2. Find guidance, don't rely on books alone. Because hormonal changes and mood swings can make consistency difficult, he recommends finding a mentor or structured programme rather than attempting the practice alone.

3. Treat the nine months as a one-time window. His closing argument is that this specific pregnancy will never come again in the same way — whatever time remains before delivery is worth using with full attention.

Garbh Sanskar Se Jude Sawal (Frequently Asked Questions — Garbh Sanskar)

What is Garbh Sanskar in simple words?
According to Dr. Vishnu Mane, Garbh Sanskar is the practice of a pregnant mother consciously staying happy, active, creative, peaceful, and positive every day throughout pregnancy, supported by daily routines rather than a one-time ritual or class.
Is Garbh Sanskar a religious practice?
Dr. Mane describes it as open to people of all faiths. He says people from many religious backgrounds, including a large number of NRIs, follow the practice, and no religious rituals are performed as part of it.
When should Garbh Sanskar ideally begin?
He suggests preparation should ideally begin around 90 days before conception. If that window is missed, starting from whichever month of pregnancy a parent is currently in is still valuable.
Can reading books alone count as Garbh Sanskar?
No. Dr. Mane repeatedly stresses this as the biggest misconception — books provide knowledge, but the practice comes from consistent daily activity, not from reading alone.
Does stress during pregnancy really affect the baby?
Dr. Mane references research linking prenatal stress to cortisol production and foetal growth effects. This reflects his understanding of research he has read, not independent medical verification.
What role does the father play?
He describes the father's role as twofold — a direct biological role, and an equally important role of creating a calm, low-conflict environment for the mother.
How can someone get started?
Dr. Mane mentions an app called Garbh Sanskar Challenge, along with online resources under his name, as ways to access structured guidance.
Garbh Sanskar se baby intelligent hota hai kya? (Does Garbh Sanskar make the baby intelligent?)
Dr. Mane argues that a child's intellectual and emotional "software" — grasping ability, IQ, EQ, and processing capacity — is largely shaped during pregnancy through the mother's daily practices. He does not claim guaranteed intelligence, but says consistent Garbh Sanskar practice during all nine months creates the conditions for higher capacity development in the womb.
Kya Garbh Sanskar ek dharmik practice hai? (Is Garbh Sanskar a religious practice?)
Dr. Mane is careful to separate Garbh Sanskar from religious ritual. He says it is often mistaken for pooja-paath, but presents it instead as a repeatable, learnable daily practice open to people of all faiths. No religious rituals are performed as part of it.
Pregnancy mein kya padhna chahiye? (What should I read during pregnancy?)
According to Dr. Mane, reading material during pregnancy should be connected to the qualities parents hope to nurture in their child. His suggestion is to read with intention — choosing books, stories, and content that align with the values and capacities you want to develop — not reading at random or purely for entertainment.
