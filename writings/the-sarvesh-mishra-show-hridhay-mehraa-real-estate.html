What to Check Before You Invest in Property: Hridhay Mehraa on India's Real Estate Reality

Is every property worth buying? Is every builder trustworthy? On The Sarvesh Mishra Show, Sarvesh Mishra sits with real estate entrepreneur Hridhay Mehraa on India’s property market: honesty in brokerage, builder credibility, infrastructure, buyer mistakes and why you should not invest only because someone is selling a dream.

Key takeaways
  • Don’t invest only because someone is selling a dream. Understand the property, location, developer and numbers first.
  • Check builder legacy and completed-project track record, not only advertisements.
  • Plots, flats and builder floors are different markets. None always “wins”.
  • Compare carpet area in official documents, not only advertised super area. Check PLC before you pay extra.
  • A cheaper deal is not automatically a better investment if developer credibility is weak.
  • These are guest views, not a buy or sell recommendation.

1. Why brokerage instead of becoming a builder?

Coming from a family with a background in real estate development, Hridhay chose brokerage rather than simply joining the family building business. After returning from the United States he first worked in marketing and watched how real estate companies valued content creators who could influence buyers. He later joined a real estate company at a starting salary of ₹15,000 and, in a short period, became the company’s sales head — according to his account.

What he says set him apart was showing both advantages and disadvantages. His test: if he were investing his own ₹1 crore, what would he want to know?

2. Big corporate players are entering

He says the industry is moving from traditional family-run developers toward larger organised corporates. He mentions companies such as Reliance, Birla, Godrej and Mahindra expanding in real estate. Those are guest mentions, not endorsements. Large brands can increase buyer confidence on under-construction stock, he argues, but higher land and development costs can also push launch prices up. Affordable housing, in his view, cannot depend on developers alone; policy, land, infrastructure and connectivity matter too.

3. Why are prices rising in Gurgaon and Noida?

He points to aspirations in Tier 2, Tier 3 and smaller cities, social media, jobs, education and lifestyle — plus infrastructure: roads, offices, schools, retail and communities. Better connectivity from Gurgaon to surrounding areas could, he says, make farther locations livable. A location’s future depends on urbanisation, employment and infrastructure, not only how it looks today. Treat city and price comments as his snapshot, not a forecast.

4. How to identify a trustworthy builder

Legacy: how long the company has operated, who founded it, reputation, completed history.

Track record: visit completed projects if you can; check construction, maintenance, facilities, Google reviews and public feedback. A famous name is not enough. Verify RERA and legal papers yourself.

5. Plots vs flats vs builder floors

Plots can appreciate when the area develops, but they depend on location, infrastructure, legal clarity and future demand. Flats, he says, can stay attractive for decades if societies are professionally maintained. Builder floors grew when large developers were not supplying enough inventory; more organised projects could change that. Don’t pick a category only because someone says it always appreciates.

6. Ready-to-move vs under-construction

Ready-to-move lets you inspect the actual home and removes much timeline uncertainty, but it may carry a premium. Under-construction can look cheaper; you still need developer credibility, delivery history and finances. His roti analogy: if a child needs two rotis and eight are cooked, excess supply sits unused. Too many launches in one category can take years to absorb. That is his illustration, not a timing signal.

7. Builder credibility vs a cheap price

Established developers may charge more because buyers trust delivery. A discount from a weaker name is not automatically a better buy. He would rather manage risk than chase the highest possible appreciation when years of savings are on the line.

8. How brokers make money — and how he wants the job done

He acknowledges brokerage can be highly profitable and that the profession needs more honesty. He prefers calling himself a broker rather than hiding behind fancy titles. Don’t sell only for commission; explain both sides. Meet people to build a relationship, not only to close a ticket. Referrals, in his view, outlast one deal.

9. Location vs a famous address

A developed pocket with schools and malls can look safe; he also asks investors to study places still developing — future roads, jobs, developers and supply. He discusses Gurgaon, including established vs developing belts, and surrounding areas such as Sohna. Future development is not a guarantee of appreciation. Ask whether there is a credible pathway. Related: Rakesh Chugh on Gurugram (a different episode).

10. Real estate is not one market

High-rise residential, commercial, industrial/warehousing, plotted land and builder floors can sit in different supply cycles. One segment can be oversupplied while another still has demand. Don’t assume every category moves together.

11. Carpet area, super area and PLC

Carpet area: usable area within internal walls under applicable RERA rules. Read the official documents, not only verbal claims. Exclusive balcony or verandah is not carpet area under that definition, he warns.

Super area and loading: the gap between super area and carpet area. Use it to compare usable space across projects.

PLC (preferential location charges): extra for floor or view. Preferences change. An expensive floor is not automatically a better investment. Confirm current RERA wording with a professional.

12. How much money before you invest?

Asked whether someone should approach a luxury broker with ₹1 crore, ₹4 crore or ₹5 crore, he challenges the idea that every customer must buy an expensive home. Don’t buy only because it fits the budget. Ask whether a genuinely suitable property exists in that budget, and whether it fits horizon and risk capacity. Affordable, luxury and commercial can sit in different demand phases.

13. Build knowledge, not just income

He says professionals should develop insights others don’t have, rather than spending most of their attention on cars, watches and holidays. Deep industry knowledge becomes value. In a business with large ticket sizes, he also talks about gratitude and focusing on the work instead of stressing every deal’s outcome.

14. The host’s close: honesty and knowledge

Sarvesh Mishra’s two lines: knowledge gives an advantage in any profession; honesty builds a reputation that compounds. He also shares a Ramcharitmanas thought — सुनहु भरत भावी प्रबल… हानि लाभु जीवनु मरनु जसु अपजसु बिधि हाथ — that gain and loss are not entirely in our control; effort and conduct are.

Frequently asked questions

Is real estate a good investment in India?

It can be a long-term opportunity, but returns depend on location, price, developer, demand, supply and infrastructure. Evaluate each property. This is not financial advice.

Should I invest in plots or flats?

No universal answer. Plots may offer land-appreciation opportunities; well-managed apartments can offer convenience, rentals and maintenance. Your objective matters.

How do I check whether a builder is trustworthy?

History, completed projects, delivery record, reviews, finances, RERA and legal documents.

Is ready-to-move safer than under-construction?

Ready-to-move lets you inspect the asset. Under-construction may price differently but adds delivery risk. Both need due diligence.

What is the difference between carpet area and super area?

Carpet area is usable area within internal walls under the applicable RERA definition. Super area is a broader marketing measure that may include a share of common areas.

What is PLC?

Preferential Location Charges — extra for certain floors, views or locations. Weigh cost against benefit and resale demand.

Why is Gurgaon often discussed as a market?

In this episode, employment, commercial development, connectivity and residential demand. Guest view, not a prediction. Different from the Rakesh Chugh Gurugram episode.

What should a first-time buyer check?

Location, developer, RERA, title, carpet area, total cost, payment schedule, maintenance and resale demand — then verify independently.

Can a broker identify a good investment?

A transparent broker can share market context. Buyers should still verify every important claim and not depend only on a recommendation.

What is the biggest lesson from his journey, in the brief?

Develop deep professional knowledge, stay honest, build relationships, and focus on the work rather than chasing money alone.
