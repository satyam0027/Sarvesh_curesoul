₹10,000 से ₹10 करोड़ का Formula? The Hidden Truth of Option Trading

Can ₹10,000 Really Become ₹10 Crore Through Trading?

The internet is full of extraordinary money-making claims. Start with ₹10,000. Trade options. Compound aggressively. Become a crorepati.

But is there really a reliable formula that can turn ₹10,000 into ₹10 crore?

In this episode of The Sarvesh Mishra Show, Sarvesh Mishra speaks with Deepak Wadhwa about options trading, market psychology, trading capital, unrealistic return expectations and the difference between learning the market and chasing quick money.

The biggest lesson is simple: there is no guaranteed shortcut from ₹10,000 to ₹10 crore. Read the show explainer at What Is The Sarvesh Mishra Show?

₹10,000 to ₹10 Crore: What Does the Mathematics Really Say?

Compounding can create large numbers over long periods. But there is an important difference between mathematical compounding and guaranteed trading returns.

If someone assumes an exceptionally high return every year without interruption, the final number can look extraordinary. The problem is that real markets do not provide a fixed return every year.

The conversation challenges the idea that someone can simply invest ₹1,000 today and reliably turn it into ₹1 lakh within a few years. The more realistic principle is: more capital + more time + disciplined investing can influence the journey, but no formula guarantees a specific outcome. Option Trading Is Not the Same as Long Term Investing

This distinction is critical. Investing generally focuses on owning assets for a longer period and participating in their potential growth. Options trading involves derivatives whose value can change rapidly and whose outcomes depend on factors such as price movement, volatility, time and the specific strategy used.

Because derivatives can provide significant exposure with relatively less upfront capital, both profits and losses can be amplified. SEBI specifically warns that derivatives can multiply profits or losses and that speculative derivatives trading can potentially cause severe financial losses. That is why options should not be presented as an easy shortcut to wealth.

The Hidden Truth Behind Quick Money

One of the strongest themes in the conversation is skepticism towards extraordinary financial promises. If someone says, “Give me ₹10,000 and I will make you ₹10 lakh,” the first question should not be “How do I join?” It should be “What is the risk?” And then: “If this method is so reliable, why isn't everyone using it?”

The episode repeatedly questions the psychology behind such claims and encourages people to think critically rather than blindly follow financial promises. See also Sagar Sinha on The Real Rich.

Why Small Money Still Matters

Starting with a small amount does not mean the money is meaningless. The conversation makes an important point: small capital can be the beginning of a learning journey.

The objective should not necessarily be to turn ₹10,000 into crores immediately. Instead, a beginner can focus on: Learn → Understand risk → Start small → Gain experience → Improve discipline. Increasing capital without increasing knowledge can increase the size of the mistake.

The Biggest Risk in Option Trading: Your Own Behaviour

Markets do not only test your strategy. They test your psychology. Fear can make traders exit too early. Greed can make them increase positions after profits. Losses can encourage revenge trading. A winning streak can create overconfidence. A losing streak can create desperation.

This is why trading is not simply about predicting whether the market will go up or down. It is also about risk management and emotional discipline.

Can You Really Make ₹10 Crore From ₹10,000?

Mathematically, any very large target can be represented through compounding assumptions. Practically, the question is much harder. To turn ₹10,000 into ₹10 crore, the money would need to multiply 1,000 times. That requires either extraordinary returns, a very long period, substantial additional capital, or some combination of these.

There is no legitimate formula that removes market risk. So instead of asking “How quickly can ₹10,000 become ₹10 crore?”, a more useful question is: “What is a realistic financial goal, how much can I invest, and what level of risk can I actually afford?”

What Does SEBI Say About F&O Risk?

SEBI's research has repeatedly highlighted the risks faced by individual traders in equity derivatives. Its FY22 to FY24 study found that 93% of individual traders incurred losses in equity F&O, with aggregate losses exceeding ₹1.8 lakh crore over the three-year period.

SEBI's investor education material also recommends understanding your investment objective and risk appetite before entering the securities market and suggests beginners learn basic investing before exploring complex instruments such as derivatives. This makes one principle particularly important: do not enter options trading because someone has shown you a large profit screenshot.

Common Option Trading Mistakes

  • Chasing quick returns. Expecting extraordinary returns in a short period can encourage excessive risk.
  • Increasing capital after a winning streak. A few successful trades do not guarantee future success.
  • Trading without a risk limit. Every trading strategy needs to account for potential losses.
  • Following tips blindly. Someone else's trade may not suit your capital, risk tolerance or financial situation.
  • Revenge trading. Trying to recover a loss immediately can turn one bad trade into several.
  • Confusing leverage with wealth. More exposure does not automatically mean more wealth.
  • Believing guaranteed returns. Market-linked products cannot offer a risk-free shortcut to extraordinary returns.
  • Ignoring opportunity cost. Money used for high-risk trading may also have been used for long-term investing, emergency savings or other financial goals.

What Should Beginners Do Before Trading Options?

Before entering derivatives, ask:

  • Do I understand how the instrument works?
  • Can I afford to lose the capital involved?
  • Do I have an emergency fund?
  • Am I trading because I understand the strategy or because someone promised quick money?
  • What is my maximum acceptable loss?
  • What happens if the trade moves against me?

These questions are more important than finding the next “multibagger” or winning option trade. More guests live in the guest index and the writings archive.

Investing, Trading or Speculation?

These three concepts should not be treated as identical. Investing focuses primarily on building wealth over time. Trading focuses on taking positions based on expected price movements. Speculation involves taking significant risk based on expectations about future price movements.

The problem begins when a person enters a high-risk trade while mentally treating it like a guaranteed investment.

The Real Lesson of the ₹10,000 to ₹10 Crore Formula

₹10,000 → ₹10 crore. But the deeper lesson from the conversation is about expectations. There is no magic formula that turns small capital into massive wealth without risk.

The more useful formula is: Knowledge + Discipline + Risk Management + Time + Realistic Expectations. If you are entering the market, understand what you are buying, understand what you can lose and never confuse a high return possibility with a guaranteed outcome.

Key Takeaways

  1. ₹10,000 cannot be assumed to become ₹10 crore through a guaranteed trading formula.
  2. Compounding works mathematically, but market returns are not fixed.
  3. Options and derivatives carry significant risk.
  4. Small capital can be useful for learning, but increasing capital also increases potential losses.
  5. Trading psychology can be as important as market knowledge.
  6. Avoid guaranteed return claims and unrealistic profit promises.
  7. Never copy another trader's strategy blindly.
  8. Understand risk before increasing exposure.
  9. Long-term wealth creation and short-term speculation are different approaches.
  10. Financial decisions should be based on your own goals and circumstances.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can ₹10,000 become ₹10 crore through option trading?
There is no guaranteed method to achieve this. Such a target would require extraordinary compounding, substantial additional capital, a very long period or a combination of these, with significant risk.
Is option trading safe for beginners?
Options trading can involve substantial risk and may result in rapid losses. Beginners should understand the instrument, risks and mechanics before considering derivatives. SEBI specifically advises investors to learn the basics before exploring complex instruments.
Can options make you rich quickly?
Large profits are possible, but large losses are also possible. A possibility of high returns should never be confused with a reliable or guaranteed outcome.
What is the biggest mistake beginners make in options?
One common mistake is entering the market primarily because of profit promises without understanding risk, position sizing and potential losses.
Is investing better than trading?
They serve different purposes. Long-term investing and active trading involve different objectives, strategies and risks. The appropriate approach depends on an individual's financial goals and circumstances.
What should I learn before trading options?
Understand options mechanics, risk, position sizing, volatility, losses, trading costs and the specific strategy being used before putting significant capital at risk.

There Is No Shortcut to ₹10 Crore

The most important question is not whether ₹10,000 can become ₹10 crore. It is whether the person taking that journey understands risk, time, discipline and reality. Options can be powerful financial instruments, but they are not a guaranteed wealth machine.

Before chasing the next big return, learn the market, understand the downside and protect the money you can afford to lose. Because in the market, surviving long enough to learn can be more valuable than chasing the fastest possible profit.
