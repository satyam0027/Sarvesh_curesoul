How to invest in Gurugram real estate, in his telling

Use a trustworthy broker, buy ready to move, consider land before a flat, borrow from a bank so its legal team double-checks the property, and treat “RERA approved” as a starting point. Verify ownership, land use and sanctioned plans before paying.

Why a trustworthy property broker matters in Delhi NCR

Chugh says a buyer with a 50 crore budget can struggle for three years to find the right property, so money alone does not get you the asset. A sound decision does, and it usually starts with a trustworthy broker. He sells only what he would use himself, and refuses builder inventory with high commissions.

Gurugram vs Delhi: is Gurugram undervalued?

He says prime Gurugram sectors cost roughly four times less than South Delhi. He notes comparable malls, offices and premium sectors, and rejects the idea that Gurugram is unsafe, pointing to its nightlife and night-shift culture. Prices change — compare current rates before you treat that multiple as a quote.

Ready to move vs under construction

His advice is firm: buy ready to move. A tailored shirt must be worn even if it fits badly, while a ready shirt lets you pick one from twenty. Under construction, you see a plan you may not fully understand and hand your future to someone else.

Plot vs flat in Gurugram

He argues land keeps appreciating while flats tend to depreciate, so a flat should not be your first property or an investment. It can suit lifestyle once you own other assets. Many buyers disagree. Treat it as his opinion, not a rule.

Is a home loan EMI a smart way to buy?

Chugh calls EMI a smart tool, not a trap. He quoted bank interest at about 7.1 to 7.2 percent at the time, cheaper than most alternatives. A bank’s legal and valuation team checks the property more rigorously than most buyers can, and he prefers a home loan from a nationalised bank for stricter checks and fewer hidden charges. Borrow only what your income comfortably covers. Those interest figures will have moved — check the current rate with your bank.

Is RERA approved safe?

RERA registration is one data point, not a guarantee, since many cases are pending. His checklist:

  • Does the builder own the land?
  • Is the change of land use approved?
  • Is the building plan sanctioned?
  • Does the seller hold a registered sale deed?
  • Does the master plan suit your purpose?
  • Is the project loanable from a bank?
Due diligence, not a logo

Treat “RERA approved” as a starting point. Pay a lawyer to run the list above. Skipping the legal check to save a fee risks the entire investment.

How to avoid property fraud

Fraud usually needs an insider who earns your trust, and greed does the rest. Be wary when everything works out too easily. Verify every document independently.

Infrastructure hype vs real jobs

Road announcements alone do not create value; jobs and industry do. Ask what is actually being built and who will live or work there.

First property on a tight budget

Make a checklist and aim for 70 to 80 percent of it. Compromise on size, not on location, legal clarity or loan eligibility. A small, clean, legal home near work beats a large one that costs you an hour of commute daily.

The five-minute quiet test

Chugh suggests sitting alone on a property for five to seven minutes in silence. If you feel at peace, buy; otherwise reconsider. This is his belief about energy and vastu, but a quiet visit costs nothing.

Real example: how a 3-lakh plot strategy built 15 houses

Chugh’s buyer purchased 60 square yard plots at about 3 lakh each, took loans on the plot and construction, and finished houses in about a year. Rent paid the EMIs while plots appreciated. He ended up with 15 houses, sold three for about 3 crore, and kept the rest. Treat it as an illustration, not a plan.

Not a template

Leverage, rent and appreciation all have to line up. Past plots at 3 lakh do not mean the same maths works at today’s Gurugram prices. Verify documents, loan eligibility and current rates before you copy any of this.
