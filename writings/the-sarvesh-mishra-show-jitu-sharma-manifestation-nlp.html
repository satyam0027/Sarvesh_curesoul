Magic Nahi, Science Hai: The 8 Real Steps of Manifestation & NLP

Guest: Coach Jitu Sharma — Manifestation & NLP Coach, founder of a 21-day subconscious reprogramming program and the Decode Identity course
Host: Sarvesh Mishra · Sarvesh Mishra Show
Category: Manifestation, NLP, Subconscious Mind, Money Mindset

This article is based on a podcast conversation featuring Coach Jitu Sharma on The Sarvesh Mishra Show. It is educational content — not medical or psychological advice. Why This Conversation Matters

"Socho aur ₹1 crore kama lo." Somewhere on the internet, someone has promised you exactly that. In this episode of The Sarvesh Mishra Show, Sarvesh Mishra puts that promise to the test with Coach Jitu Sharma, a manifestation and NLP coach, around a simple question viewers kept sending in: if manifestation is real, why doesn't it work for everyone who tries it?

The answer, as the episode unpacks live — with demonstrations, not just theory — is that manifestation is not a wish. It is an 8-stage process, and most people quietly skip five of the eight stages without realising it. Read the show explainer at What Is The Sarvesh Mishra Show?

What Does Manifestation Actually Mean?

According to Coach Jitu Sharma, manifestation isn't a modern invention or a mystical shortcut — it's something every person has been doing unconsciously since childhood. He offers a simple example: a child at school sees another child's bicycle and thinks, "I should have one too." That thought is a "chaht" (a desire). The journey from that desire to owning an actual bicycle — asking a parent, building belief, taking the right action — is manifestation in its complete form.

The confusion, he explains, comes from how manifestation is taught online today: most content stops at visualise it, make a vision board, feel it as done. According to Jitu Sharma, that's real, but it's only the fourth of eight stages — and skipping the first three and the last four is exactly why so many people feel manifestation "doesn't work" for them.

The 8 Stages of Manifestation — Explained Through a Farmer's Field

StageFarmer's fieldManifestation equivalent
1Choosing a good seedThe desire itself (soul-based or ego-based)
2Preparing and tilling the soilClearing old subconscious beliefs
3Sowing the seedSetting a clear intention
4Germination — sunlight and waterAffirmation (sunlight) + visualization (watering)
5Protecting the crop from birdsAligned action, staying focused on the one goal
6Surviving storms and hailHolding belief through fear and setbacks
7The crop begins to flowerStaying grounded as results start appearing
8Harvest and sale at the marketThe result — and the discipline to manage it well

Coach Jitu Sharma is direct about the reason most manifestation attempts fail at scale: people practice stage 4 — affirmation and visualization — in isolation, without ever building the seed, the soil, or the belief underneath it. As he puts it, some people get a result 5–10% of the time through luck and call it "manifestation," and when it doesn't repeat, they conclude manifestation itself is fake — much like saying cricket isn't a real sport because most players never get selected for the national team.

Soul-Based Desire vs Ego-Based Desire

One of the sharpest distinctions Jitu Sharma draws is between soul-based desire and ego-based desire — and he argues this single choice, right at "seed" stage, decides whether the rest of the process even has a chance to work.

He illustrates it with an example from the episode itself: someone watching Sarvesh Mishra's well-built studio might think, out of envy, "I want a bigger studio than his." That is an ego-based desire — it's rooted in comparison and competition. A soul-based version of the same desire would sound different: "I want to build something that helps people the way Sarvesh Mishra's platform helps his audience."

According to the episode, even if an ego-based desire is technically "manifested," it tends to collapse — the person eventually comes back down — while a soul-based desire keeps pulling a person forward, even after the initial goal is reached. Related: Sagar Sinha on The Real Rich.

Why "Socho Aur Pao" Thinking Creates More Frustration

Host Sarvesh Mishra raises a pointed question early on: does pure visualization and affirmation — the "think it and get it" version of manifestation — actually work on its own? Jitu Sharma's answer is blunt: not only does it not work in isolation, it actively creates frustration, because a person keeps writing affirmations and visualizing without result, and the gap between the words and the reality compounds their disappointment.

His alternative isn't to abandon affirmation and visualization — it's to place them correctly inside the 8-stage sequence, and to change the state of mind in which they're practiced.

The Affirmation and Visualization Technique — Step by Step

This is the portion of the episode that moves from theory into a live, guided exercise. According to Jitu Sharma, affirmations only reach the subconscious mind when the conscious mind — which judges and argues — is quieted first, ideally in an alpha or theta brain state, not the normal, alert beta state most people are in when they write affirmations on their phone.

  1. Settle and observe the breath. Sit with eyes closed, and simply notice thoughts arriving and leaving, without engaging them — like clouds passing across the sky.
  2. Deep belly breathing (3 rounds). Inhale fully into the stomach; exhale slowly, imagining a dark smoke of negativity leaving the body.
  3. Sound anchoring. A few silent repetitions of "Om," followed by five repetitions of "So-Ham," while attention stays on the breath.
  4. Close the sequence. Rub the palms together, place them gently over the face, and open the eyes with a soft smile — the mind is now in a calmer, more receptive state.
  5. Only now, write the affirmation. With the conscious "gatekeeper" quieted, the affirmation is written directly toward the subconscious mind, without the usual internal argument.
  6. Follow with visualization. Specific sensory detail — colour, sound, feeling — is added to whatever is being visualised, mirroring what's just been affirmed.

According to the episode, this is typically run as a 40–45 minute guided session, and the instruction afterward is strict: no phone, no distraction, roughly 10 minutes of uninterrupted affirmation writing immediately after. The Sadhu and the Flood — Why Belief Without Action Isn't Manifestation

Jitu Sharma tells a well-known parable to make one final point about belief: a respected sadhu in a village refuses to leave during a flood, insisting God will save him. A boat comes — he refuses it. A second boat comes — he refuses it. A helicopter lowers a rope — he refuses that too, and he drowns. Meeting God afterward, he asks why he wasn't saved despite his faith. God's reply: "Who do you think sent the boats and the helicopter? You had to take the action."

The point, according to Jitu Sharma, is that belief and manifestation aren't a substitute for action — belief is what should make a person recognise and take the opportunity that shows up, not wait passively for a miracle. Related: Ashish Agarwal on starting a business with ₹1 lakh.

Manifestation vs NLP — What's the Difference?

Host Sarvesh Mishra asks whether manifestation and NLP (Neuro-Linguistic Programming) are the same thing under different names. According to Jitu Sharma, they overlap heavily but aren't identical — both are structured processes, not magic, and both work on emotion, belief and behaviour. NLP, specifically, works on:

  • Neuro — the nervous system, how we register experience through the five senses
  • Linguistic — the internal language we use about ourselves ("I'm not good enough," "I look bad")
  • Programming — the patterns this internal language creates over time, and how they can be consciously rewritten

The Classroom Study — How Labels Program Behaviour

Jitu Sharma references a classroom experiment to illustrate how quickly conditioning takes hold: two groups of average-performing students were formed, and their teacher was told one group was "the class's brightest students" and the other was simply "average." The episode uses this to show how labels and expectations can shape behaviour. Related: Gurnandan Singh on brain optimization.

The TV Technique for Fear and Phobia

The episode includes a live-demonstrated visualization for releasing fear or phobia — such as stage fright or fear of water. According to Jitu Sharma, the subconscious mind doesn't process language the way the conscious mind does — it responds to image, sound and feeling. Changing the visual "storage" of a fear memory — its colour, size and ending — is what allows the emotional charge attached to it to release.

In the demonstration, the memory is mentally shrunk, discoloured and symbolically destroyed. Then a new, larger image appears — showing the person confidently doing the thing they once feared (public speaking, swimming), with visible detail: what they're wearing, how they move, people applauding. The instruction is to sit with that feeling fully before slowly opening the eyes.

This is a coaching demonstration from the episode, not a clinical treatment. Anyone whose fear, phobia or belief pattern is significantly affecting daily functioning should consult a qualified professional.

Money Mindset and NLP's "Money Master" Program

Asked whether mindset can genuinely affect a person's earning capacity, Jitu Sharma's answer is unambiguous: yes — and he connects it to a broader NLP framework he calls Money Mastery, alongside similar frameworks for relationships. His core argument is that most people unconsciously repel money and opportunity through negative self-talk about their job or their income, comparing it to how a child who is constantly told "you're not useful" tends to avoid the person who says it, while a child who is spoken to warmly gravitates closer.

He also shares an example about the emotional "frequency" people attach to lending or spending money — describing how framing money leaving your hands as loss versus abundance changes what tends to come back. Related: Sanjay Kathuria on SIP and wealth and Raghav Kumar Garg on becoming debt free.

Why Do Some People Advance Faster Than Others With the Same Skills?

Host Sarvesh Mishra raises a familiar workplace scenario: two people with identical talent, in the same role, and one keeps getting promoted while the other stays stuck. According to Jitu Sharma, this comes down to interpretation — the internal meaning a person assigns to their circumstances. He illustrates this with two brothers raised by the same alcoholic, abusive father: one grows up choosing the same destructive path, interpreting it as "this is how things are done"; the other grows up rejecting it entirely, becoming a scientist, having interpreted the same environment as "this is what not to do." Same household, same conditioning exposure — different internal interpretation, different life.

Can NLP or Manifestation Bring Back a Relationship?

Responding to a viewer question about whether NLP techniques can bring back an ex-partner, Jitu Sharma reframes the question rather than answering it directly: manifestation and NLP aren't about pulling a specific person back — they're about building the qualities and "frequency" that attract a compatible, values-aligned relationship in the first place. He cautions against desiring someone purely for surface qualities without the financial or emotional readiness to sustain that kind of relationship.

Why Has Demand for Manifestation and NLP Grown So Much Recently?

Sarvesh Mishra asks why manifestation and NLP coaching have exploded in popularity now. Jitu Sharma's view: modern life has eroded emotional regulation and patience ("sabuuri") — small triggers, like a car cutting someone off in traffic, now produce disproportionate reactions, whereas earlier generations had more community reassurance and more tolerance for delayed results. He's careful to note that manifestation itself isn't new — he references the imagining of flight before aircraft existed, and Lord Rama's forces conceiving of a bridge to Lanka before building it — but what has changed is a demand for immediate results without the patience or consistent action the process actually requires.

Common Mistakes People Make With Manifestation and NLP

  1. Treating stage 4 as the whole process. Affirmation and visualization alone, without belief-clearing, intention and aligned action, is only one of eight stages.
  2. Choosing an ego-based desire. Desires built on competition or comparison tend to collapse even when technically achieved.
  3. Writing affirmations in a fully alert, judgmental state of mind. Without quieting the conscious mind first, the affirmation rarely reaches the subconscious.
  4. Mistaking belief for a substitute for action. As the sadhu story illustrates, opportunities still have to be recognised and taken.
  5. Giving up after one failed attempt and generalising "manifestation doesn't work." According to the episode, old belief patterns typically take 10–21 consistent days to shift — not one session.

Before You Start: A Practical Checklist

  • Identify whether your desire is soul-based or ego-based
  • Write down the specific old belief you want to change, using the four-line method discussed in the episode
  • Ask whether that belief is a "universal truth" or something you inherited
  • Choose a quiet, distraction-free space and practice the breath-based technique before writing affirmations
  • Pair every affirmation with a specific, sensory visualization
  • Define one concrete, aligned action tied to your goal
  • Track your practice for at least 21 consistent days before judging results
  • Consult a qualified professional if a fear, phobia or belief pattern is significantly affecting daily functioning

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the exact definition of manifestation according to Jitu Sharma?
Manifestation is the journey from a thought or desire ("chaht") into reality, moving through belief and aligned action. According to the episode, it is a complete 8-stage process — not simply visualizing or affirming a desired outcome.
Is manifestation the same as the Law of Attraction?
The episode treats "Law of Attraction," "manifestation," and "thoughts create reality" as overlapping popular framings of the same underlying idea, but argues that most explanations of it online are incomplete, covering only the visualization and affirmation stage rather than the full process.
Why doesn't manifestation work for most people who try it?
According to Jitu Sharma, most people only practice the affirmation-and-visualization stage without building the belief, clearing old subconscious blocks, or taking aligned action — so results are inconsistent, and inconsistent results get incorrectly labelled as "manifestation doesn't work."
What is the difference between soul-based and ego-based desire?
A soul-based desire is rooted in genuine purpose or service (wanting to help others the way a role model does); an ego-based desire is rooted in comparison or envy (wanting to outdo someone else). The episode argues soul-based desires sustain results, while ego-based ones tend to collapse.
When should affirmations and visualization actually be practiced?
According to Jitu Sharma, they should be practiced after using a breathing-based technique to move the mind out of an alert, judgmental "beta" state and into a calmer alpha or theta state, so the affirmation can reach the subconscious mind without being argued down by the conscious mind.
What is the TV Technique used for?
It's a guided visualization technique demonstrated live in the episode for releasing fear or phobia — such as stage fright or fear of water — by mentally shrinking, discolouring, and symbolically destroying the memory associated with the fear, then replacing it with a confident, resolved image.
Can NLP really change a person's earning capacity?
According to the episode, mindset strongly influences earning capacity through the self-talk a person carries about money and their work — Jitu Sharma connects this to an NLP framework he calls Money Mastery, alongside similar work on relationships.
How long does it take to change a subconscious belief?
The episode cites roughly 10 to 21 days of consistent practice to shift a specific limiting belief, depending on how deeply rooted it is.
What is Decode Identity?
Decode Identity is described in the episode as Jitu Sharma's coaching program for identifying and rewriting the core identity-level beliefs (the words a person uses after "I am") that shape thoughts, actions, and results.
Is NLP only useful for major fears or phobias?
No — the episode discusses NLP's application across confidence, workplace communication, parenting, relationships, and money mindset, not only clinical-level fears.
Can manifestation techniques bring back a specific ex-partner?
Jitu Sharma reframes this rather than endorsing it directly — the guidance in the episode is to focus on becoming a values-aligned, "frequency-matched" partner and attracting a compatible relationship, rather than targeting a specific individual.
Why has interest in manifestation and NLP grown recently?
According to the episode, modern life has reduced people's emotional patience and regulation, creating demand for tools that promise faster results — even though the underlying process still requires the same consistency it always has.

Final Takeaway

Manifestation and NLP aren't competing systems, and neither is magic — both are structured processes that work on the same raw material: belief, emotion and repeated action. The 8-stage manifestation framework and the subconscious-reprogramming tools shared in this episode only work when they're used in full, not as a single affirmation written once and abandoned after a week. As Jitu Sharma puts it through the wiring analogy, the same current that lights up a home can also cause a short circuit — the technique isn't the variable, how correctly and consistently it's applied is.
