An honest beginning

How a young reporter became someone who reads life itself.

Long before the companies and the shows, he was a young man with a notebook, a microphone, and an unreasonable need to get close to the truth — closer than the headline allowed.

The newsroom taught him technique. The field taught him everything else. Disaster zones, polling booths, hospital corridors, riot-torn neighbourhoods, courtrooms, and quiet villages tucked into India's interior — these became his classrooms. He saw what people are made of when the lights go off, the cameras pack up, and only the human remains.

What follows isn't a portfolio. It's a path. A few of the moments — among hundreds — that shaped the lens he now uses as a journalist and entrepreneur.

What the field gave him

The six quiet lessons that became a worldview.

Listen first

Before the question, the silence. The field taught him that what people don't say is often the real story.

Stay close

Truth has a distance. Get too far, you generalise. Get too close, you absorb. The field taught the right proximity.

Doubt the headline

If a story is too clean, something is being cut out. Every neat narrative hides a messier human reality.

Respect the witness

The person in front of you is not content. They are a life. Every camera-on moment is a responsibility.

Watch the inside

The most important story in any disaster, election, or court is what's happening inside the people involved — not outside.

Carry it lightly

If the field doesn't change you, you weren't really there. If it breaks you, you didn't carry it right.

"The field doesn't teach you what to think.
It teaches you what to see."

— Sarvesh Mishra

From the field to the framework

How twenty years of ground reporting became a body of work on conscious living.

Somewhere between a disaster site and a courtroom, between a polling booth and a hospital ICU, a private question kept growing: What is actually running these humans on the inside?

That question outgrew journalism. It became The Sarvesh Mishra Show, The Inner Wealth, The Urban Sannyasi, and the founding of CureSoulLife — long-form research and conversation about identity, pressure, meaning, and inner clarity.

The reporter became a researcher. The microphone became a method. But the foundation never moved — it's still the field. It's still the people. It's still the question.
